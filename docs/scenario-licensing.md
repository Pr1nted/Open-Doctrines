# What a scenario may be built from

A scenario is a small JSON file (`tools/data/scenarios/<id>.json`) that assigns
the base map's provinces to powers. It ships inside a `.odmap` that this project
distributes, so what goes into one is a licensing question and not only a design
one. This is the reasoning applied so far, written down because the same
questions keep arriving.

## The short version

| what | may we | why |
|---|---|---|
| A historical situation | yes | Facts and dates are not anyone's property. |
| A *premise* from fiction ("the Central Powers won") | yes | Ideas are not protected; only expression is. |
| Short names of places or states | generally yes | Copyright does not reach names or titles. |
| A named work's prose, slogans, characters, artwork | **no** | That is the expression, and it is protected. |
| Another game or mod's original setting | **no** | Their invented nations, characters, flags and lore are theirs. |

Choosing a licence for *this* repository grants nobody rights to somebody
else's work. If material cannot be used, the answer is to build an original
scenario on the same public premise — not to relabel it.

## The three superstates (`1984`)

Built from the premise of George Orwell's *Nineteen Eighty-Four*: the world
divided between three powers that cannot defeat one another, fighting over a
belt of contested countries between them.

What is used is the partition — an idea — and three short names. No text from
the novel appears in the scenario, in the game, or in this repository, and the
flags are procedural patterns generated from each power's colour, so no artwork
is copied. The scenario is named for what it is rather than after the book.

On the copyright status itself, because it is not uniform: Orwell died in 1950,
so the novel has been public domain in the UK, the EU and most of the world
since 1 January 2021. In the **United States** it was published in 1949 and
remains in copyright until 2045. That difference is why the scenario leans on
the least protectable elements — a geographic configuration and three names —
rather than on anything Orwell wrote.

## Scenarios inspired by other games and mods

Two are asked for often: *Kaiserreich* and *The New Order: Last Days of Europe*,
both mods for Paradox games.

**Their content cannot be used.** A fan mod is not public domain. Its invented
nations, its character rosters, its flags, its written lore and its artwork are
the original work of the people who made it, and being a mod of another
company's game adds a second set of terms on top. Copying any of that into a
game this project distributes would infringe, and no licence chosen here changes
that. Asking the authors is the only route to using their work as theirs.

**Their premises can be.** "The Central Powers won the First World War" and "the
Axis won the Second and then turned on itself" are alternate-history premises
that long predate both mods and belong to nobody. A scenario built on either is
fine provided it is genuinely ours: our own borders, our own naming, our own
flags, our own description. It must not be presented as, named after, or
advertised as compatible with somebody else's mod, because at that point the
association is the thing being borrowed.

The practical test used here: if the scenario would lose its point when every
proper noun is replaced with one of ours, the borrowing is in the expression and
the scenario should not be built. If it survives, what was taken was the idea.

## Maps that are not Earth

An invented world raises none of this, which is worth remembering when the
answer above is no. Source data still has terms — NASA and USGS planetary
elevation products are public domain, which is why they are the ones used — but
what is built on top is this project's own.
