1.3.4b - eight calendars, Rome in 50 BC, and every exotic port back on its feet

Hello everyone, 1.3.4b is out. This is a follow-up update to 1.3.3b and
preparing ourselves for 1.4.0b. This update concentrates on more QOL features,
enjoy! Here is everything.


## Tell the time in eight calendars

The in-game date can now be shown in eight different calendars, switched from
Settings -> Display:

- BC/AD and BCE/CE
- *Ab urbe condita* - years from the founding of Rome
- the Chinese sexagenary cycle
- Kōki - the Japanese imperial calendar
- Juche
- the Hebrew calendar
- the Holocene era

Pick one and the whole HUD counts in it. They are all translated, like
everything else.


## Rome, 50 BC

A new map ships with the dating systems: the Roman Republic at its greatest
extent, the year Caesar stood on the far bank of the Rubicon. The borders are
real, drawn from OpenHistoricalMap, and it comes with complete data and its own
preview in the map browser. Set the date system to *Ab urbe condita* and play
it the way a Roman would have read the year.


## The AI settles the empty quarters

The AI now colonises unclaimed territory. Blank regions of the map fill in over
a campaign instead of sitting empty forever, so a long game looks lived-in by
the end.


## Watch the AI think

Turn on Debug Mode (Settings -> Display) and then AI Debug (Settings ->
Advanced), and press F8 in a game. Three panels open:

- a running feed of every decision the AI made this turn - which country, which
  module (war, navy, economy, politics) and what it chose;
- a decision trace for the entry under the cursor, with the value the network
  put on it;
- a neural-activity view that replays a decision and shows the network itself,
  its input, hidden, embedding and attention-head layers lighting up cell by
  cell as it decides.

It is a look straight into Parrot Zero's head, and the groundwork for the
network tools coming in 1.4.0b.


## Random events

A world can be created with random events (Experimental). They are
deterministic: the same seed always produces the same run, so a world you share
plays out identically for whoever opens it.

Three ship to start with, all of them good news for the province they land on:

- **Gold Rush** - a gold-bearing province with a decent population strikes it
  rich, and the treasury swells.
- **Bumper Harvest** - a populous province has a record year and draws families
  in; its population grows.
- **Boom Town** - an industrial province pulls in workers and trade, and both
  its coffers and its population rise.

The event pack is plain data (the od-events/1 format), so a map or a mod can
ship its own.


## Modding: GDTL, and the Shaders SDK

- **The Greater Diplomacy translation layer ships enabled.** Convert a map to
  and from Greater Diplomacy 5 and from Unciv, both directions.
- **Gearbox SDK 1.6 adds the Shaders capability.** A mod names a target - the
  map, or its own panels - and picks from the host's built-in screen effects at
  a strength it chooses; the host does the compositing. A mod never touches a
  framebuffer or a shader, so it cannot draw an arbitrary image over the game,
  the interface always draws on top, and the host slews each effect toward its
  target so nothing can strobe the screen. A player can switch screen effects
  off entirely. Every 1.5 mod keeps working untouched.


## Fixes

- **Every exotic port builds again.** DOS, the PlayStation Vita and the Nintendo
  3DS had broken over the last cycle's toolchain churn - all three are green and
  shipping. The Vita one was a buried PIC-relocation problem the Vita's ELF
  tooling could not digest; it is sorted.
- **The web build is fixed**, and so are the AI correspondents - the in-game
  campaign mail talks to its model again.
- The Roman map ships with complete data and a correct preview.


Pick the build for your system from the download list. If you are on one of the
homebrew ports - Vita, 3DS, DOS - we would love to hear how it runs.

See you on the map.
