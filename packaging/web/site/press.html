Descriptions, ready to paste

Three lengths. Use whichever fits, edit freely, no attribution needed.

One line

A free grand strategy game about running a country — its industry, armies, research and politics — that plays in a browser with no download and no account.

Short — about 50 words

OpenDoctrines is a free grand strategy game in the mould of Hearts of Iron and Victoria: 1,641 provinces, six historical scenarios, and a country to run through industry, research, diplomacy, war and rebellion. It plays in a browser with nothing to install, and on Windows, macOS, Linux and Android.

Long — about 120 words

OpenDoctrines is a free grand strategy game built by one developer. It gives you a country on a 1,641-province world map and asks you to run it: the industry that pays for the army, the research that decides what the army is, the doctrines you commit to and then live with, and the neighbours, minorities and creditors who all want something.

Six historical scenarios ship with it, along with a map editor, multiplayer that needs no port forwarding, and a mod SDK that takes mods written in thirteen languages. It runs on Windows, macOS, Linux and Android, and — unusually for the genre — in a browser tab with no download and no account, which means it can be tried in about ten seconds.

Things worth knowing

Angles that are actually true, if you are looking for one.

  • A grand strategy game that runs in a browser tab. The genre normally means a multi-gigabyte download and an hour before you have done anything. This one starts in seconds, on a school Chromebook or a phone, with no account.
  • It plays inside a Discord voice channel. Launched as an Activity, everybody in the channel is in the same game, and the invite is a click.
  • Twenty-one languages, from a solo developer. Including Arabic and Urdu, which means right-to-left layout was done properly rather than skipped.
  • The AI is trained, not scripted, and its development is written up in public — several hundred journal entries recording what was tried, what was measured, and which hypotheses turned out to be wrong.
  • Mods are compiled, not interpreted scripts. The SDK takes Go, Rust, Zig, C and others, compiled to WebAssembly and run in a sandbox.
  • It is free in a way that has no second shoe. No advertising, no telemetry in the game, no cosmetics, no paid version waiting.

State of it

The game is an alpha and is labelled one everywhere. It is playable start to finish, has a tutorial, and people finish campaigns in it.

What the word is carrying: three of the five platforms are not qualified. macOS on arm64 is the one the test suite has been run on end to end; Windows, Linux and the browser build are compiled by CI and targeted, and "it compiled" is not "somebody played it". The AI plays a full game and is about level with the scripted opponent it is measured against — a rung it has reached and not passed. The tutorial has never been watched over the shoulder of somebody new to the genre, which is the only test of a tutorial that counts.

What it is not carrying, since an earlier version of this page said otherwise: saves are stable. The format is versioned and compatible in both directions by design — an older build reads a newer save and gets the right numbers — and no release has broken one.
