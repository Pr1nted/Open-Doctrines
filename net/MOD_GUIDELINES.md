# Publishing a mod on OpenDoctrines

Version: 2026-09-15

You agree to this once before your first listing, and again if it changes. It is
short on purpose.

## What this place is

A directory. You publish a **listing** — a name, a page describing the mod, and
a link to where the `.odmod` can be downloaded. **We do not host your file.** It
stays on your server, your release page, your repository, wherever you put it.

That means two things worth knowing up front:

- If your link dies, your listing stops working, and we will not have a copy.
- You can change what is behind that link at any time without telling us.

The second one is why the rules below exist.

## The rules

**Publish your own work.** If you did not write it, you need the author's
permission, and their name on the page. Reposting somebody else's mod is the
fastest way to lose the ability to publish here.

**The download link is the mod, and stays the mod.** The link must fetch the
`.odmod` you described, and it must keep doing that. Pointing it at something
else later — a different mod, an installer, an ad wall, anything — is the one
rule that gets a listing removed without discussion.

**Declare the real hash.** The SHA-256 you publish must be the digest of the
file at that link. It is what the security check is run against, and what a
careful player compares after downloading.

**Say what it does, including the awkward parts.** If your mod changes how turns
resolve, says so. If it needs another mod, say so. `modules` in your manifest is
shown on your listing, so people can see what it asked the game for.

**No malware, obviously.** Also no crypto miners, no telemetry the player is not
told about, and no `updateUrl` that exists to count who is running your mod
without saying that is what it does.

**Nothing illegal, and nothing sexual.** This game is played by school classes.

**One listing per mod.** Update the one you have; do not publish it five times
with different names to sit higher on the page.

## What we do

We look up your declared hash with a third-party scanner and show what comes
back. **A clean result is not an endorsement** — it means nobody has reported
those particular bytes yet. We say that on the listing too, because a badge that
reads "clean" and means "unknown" is worse than no badge.

We count how many people follow your download link, and show you the number.

We take reports. If a moderator agrees with one, your listing comes down; if the
problem is you rather than one mod, your ability to publish is withdrawn while
your account carries on working normally everywhere else in the game.

## What we do not do

We do not check that your mod works, that it is any good, or that it is safe. We
have not run it. Nobody here has.

We do not take a cut, because there is nothing to take a cut of, and we do not
sell anything about the people who download your mod. The only numbers we keep
are how many times your link was followed, and roughly how many different people
followed it each day.

## If you stop

Withdraw a listing whenever you like; it disappears from the directory and its
counts go with it. Deleting your account takes your listings with it.

## The part that is the game's, not ours

The game will never download or install a mod on its own, from here or from
anywhere. A player fetches your file and adds it themselves. That is a deliberate
decision in the game's sandbox, and a directory is not a reason to change it —
so nothing you publish here is ever fetched and run by somebody's game without
them doing it.
